# Philosophy Degree Salary: What Graduates Earn After College

Four years after completing a philosophy bachelor's degree, the measured graduates of UC Santa Barbara had median annual earnings of **$71,796**. At UC Santa Cruz, the figure was **$51,398**. That **$20,398 spread** within one public university system gives applicants a reason to examine what happens after graduation alongside what they will study.[¹](#source-1)

The Department of Education's College Scorecard lets us compare earnings and federal student borrowing at six UC philosophy programs, with the number of people behind each figure. The national benchmark puts those campus results in context.

## Six philosophy programs, earnings and debt together

The earnings figures below follow people who completed in 2017–18 or 2018–19, measured four years later and adjusted to **2024 dollars**. They cover federally aided graduates who worked and were not enrolled during measurement. The debt figures describe a separate, newer group of borrowers completing in 2022–23 or 2023–24.[²](#source-2)[³](#source-3)

| Philosophy bachelor's program | Median annual earnings | Earnings count | Median student federal debt | Borrower count |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| UC Berkeley | $63,326 | 67 | $12,891 | 80 |
| UC Davis | $58,633 | 38 | $16,216 | 46 |
| UCLA | $61,754 | 104 | $11,337 | 84 |
| UC Riverside | $51,402 | 47 | $15,944 | 49 |
| UC Santa Barbara | $71,796 | 73 | $11,599 | 68 |
| UC Santa Cruz | $51,398 | 46 | $15,178 | 34 |

The reported debt is student federal loan borrowing, including borrowing at other institutions attended. It excludes private loans and Parent PLUS loans. It is neither the full price of the degree nor the balance graduates still owe today.[³](#source-3)

These are all six UC groups meeting the earnings-count rule; debt availability did not determine inclusion. Irvine, San Diego and Merced also appear in the source, but their four-year earnings and earnings counts are privacy-suppressed.[⁴](#source-4)

## A $54,455 median, with widely different outcomes

The Scorecard national benchmark is **$54,455**, based on **6,085 measured workers**. Its middle half spans **$34,926 to $78,043**, an interval of $43,117 between the lower and upper quartiles. A single average-sounding figure conceals a substantial range of outcomes.[¹](#source-1)

## Four UC campuses sit above the national benchmark

Santa Barbara has the highest reported median among these six groups. Berkeley, UCLA and Davis also report medians above the national benchmark. These are observed differences between groups, rather than estimates of what choosing one campus would do to the same student's pay.

![Four-year philosophy bachelor's earnings for six UC program groups, with disclosed earnings counts. UC Santa Barbara reports $71,796, Berkeley $63,326, UCLA $61,754, Davis $58,633, Riverside $51,402 and Santa Cruz $51,398.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prepsavvy/prepsavvy-blog/main/figures/philosophy-outcomes/2026-10-27/uc-program-earnings.png)

The debt column adds another useful observation. Santa Barbara's reported median student federal debt is $11,599, while Davis reports $16,216. Published earnings and published borrowing do not move together in a simple order. Since those measures concern different cohorts, dividing one by the other would not describe a graduate's return on investment.

## What to ask before choosing a program

An applicant can use these numbers to build a shortlist, then investigate what each program offers: courses, faculty, writing and research opportunities, internships and career support. Ask the university for recent graduate destinations and how it helps students connect their academic work to employment or further study.

Then compare the price you would actually pay after aid. A published loan median describes past borrowers; it does not replace your financial-aid offer, living costs or expected borrowing. Put those personal figures beside the program's academic fit when you [compare schools](/schools).

A strong program has to fit both your academic ambitions and the price you can afford. Use these figures to sharpen that decision, then ask each university to show you the opportunities behind its degree.
