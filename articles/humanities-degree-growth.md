# Humanities Degrees Fell 14%. These Public Universities Grew.

The national figures give students considering the humanities plenty to worry about. Across the subjects in our analysis, U.S. universities awarded 12,290 fewer bachelor's degrees in 2024 than in 2021. English, history, languages, philosophy and religious studies all lost ground.

Individual campuses tell a more interesting story. At California State University, San Bernardino, humanities awards rose 35%. At San Diego State, they rose 26%. At Middle Tennessee State, they rose 39%, with English awards doubling.

For a student choosing where to study, those differences matter. A national decline says little about the size and direction of the department they would actually join. We used federal degree records to identify public universities moving against that decline, then examined the subjects behind their growth.[¹](#source-1)

## The public universities with the largest increases

PrepSavvy compared first-major bachelor's awards in 2021 and 2024 across languages, English and writing, philosophy and religious studies, history, and humanistic studies. Nationally, awards in these subjects fell from **87,916 to 75,626, a 14% decline**, while all bachelor's awards fell 5%.

The largest increases came from two California public universities. Their combined gain of 147 awards occurred while the national total was falling across every subject group we examined.

## English and history explain most of the growth

At **CSU San Bernardino**, general English awards increased from 93 to 126. History rose from 70 to 114 when general and public history are combined. Those two subject groups supplied 77 additional awards, equal to the university's entire net humanities increase. Languages added seven; philosophy lost seven. The current catalog lists English, history, public history and philosophy degrees.[²](#source-2)

At **San Diego State**, English and writing added 43 awards, and history added 25. Together, they account for 68 of the university's 70 additional humanities awards. The gain was concentrated in established disciplines rather than spread evenly across every humanities subject. SDSU also offers a separate humanities major with global and European emphases.[³](#source-3)

At **Middle Tennessee State**, general English doubled from 48 to 96 awards. One additional writing award brought the English-and-writing increase to 49, accounting for 86% of the university's net humanities gain. History added ten. MTSU's current English program includes literary studies, writing, linguistics and teaching pathways.[⁴](#source-4)

![Subject changes at the three leading public universities: English and history supply most of the additional awards, with smaller gains and declines elsewhere.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prepsavvy/prepsavvy-blog/main/figures/humanities-growth/2026-10-26/subject-growth.png)

These are different campus stories with a shared pattern: English and history were gaining graduates in places where the broader national narrative suggests retreat.

## Growth means more when the humanities gain share

A university can graduate more humanities majors simply because it is graduating more students overall. Comparing the humanities with the rest of the institution helps separate those patterns.

At all three leading public universities, the humanities grew faster than total bachelor's awards. Their share rose from **5.5% to 6.8% at CSU San Bernardino**, **3.2% to 3.9% at San Diego State**, and **3.7% to 4.1% at Middle Tennessee State**.

Yale illustrates the other pattern. Its humanities awards increased from 175 to 227, up 30%. Total bachelor's awards grew faster, from 1,197 to 1,665. The humanities therefore represented a smaller share of the graduating class: **14.6% to 13.6%**. Both statements are true: more humanities awards, a smaller slice of the university's total.[¹](#source-1)

![Humanities gained share at CSU San Bernardino, San Diego State and Middle Tennessee State; Yale's share fell despite a higher award count.](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prepsavvy/prepsavvy-blog/main/figures/humanities-growth/2026-10-26/humanities-share.png)

The comparison begins in a pandemic-affected year. These two graduating classes establish a change between endpoints; they do not establish uninterrupted annual growth or explain its cause. Annual records and current department information are the next evidence to examine.

## Choose the department you will actually join

Students considering the humanities have reasons to look beyond the familiar names. A regional public university can have a growing English or history program even while the national total falls. The subject breakdown can also reveal that a university's apparent humanities growth is happening somewhere other than the major a student wants.

Use the numbers to build a shortlist, then examine the courses offered this year, the faculty teaching them, and the opportunities for research, writing and internships. For students focused on scholarship, our [philosophy research rankings](/blog/top-philosophy-schools) and [full research comparison](/research/philosophy) address another part of that decision: where influential work is being produced.

The strongest choice brings those questions together: a department with the expertise you want, a substantial community studying your subject, and opportunities that connect the degree to the work you hope to do.
