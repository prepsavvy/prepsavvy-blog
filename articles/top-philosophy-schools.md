# Top Philosophy Schools Worldwide for Research and Influence

Philosophy majors have spent years answering the same question: what are you going to do with that degree?

One answer now comes from the companies building artificial intelligence. Anthropic is advertising a four-month Conceptual Reasoning Fellowship paying $100,000-$167,000. Philosophy is among the disciplines it recruits, and applicants must hold or be completing a relevant PhD, or have equivalent research experience. One proposed assignment is to develop difficult problems that test Claude's judgment in ethics or formal epistemology. [Anthropic fellowship](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5447080008)

Google DeepMind has also advertised a Research Scientist, Philosophy and Human Values role, explicitly welcoming a philosophy PhD or equivalent practical experience. Its work concerns questions about advanced AI, ethics and alignment. [Google's posting](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/139822896062046918-research-scientist-philosophy-and-human-values-deepmind)

Those jobs put a practical purpose behind questions philosophers have debated for centuries. What counts as knowledge? Who is responsible for a decision? Which interests should take priority when they conflict? Companies deploying AI systems have to make choices about those questions in the products people use.

For students considering philosophy, the useful next question is where influential scholarship is happening, and which research communities are building momentum.

PrepSavvy compared 23,457 indexed articles published between 2015 and 2024 in 31 philosophy journals. The comparison covers universities worldwide, with 112 institutions meeting the threshold of 50 articles. Oxford leads the research influence index. UC Irvine is the highest-ranked U.S. institution, followed by NYU.

## The research rankings

The influence score counts contributions to articles in the most-cited tenth of the same journal and publication year. One point represents a highly cited article credited entirely to one university; shared papers and citation ties receive fractional credit. Attributed citations also share credit across universities and are rounded to whole numbers. Research growth compares 2015–2019 with 2020–2024. The completion columns show one year of U.S. degree awards in philosophy and related programs, classified in CIP family 38.01. They count degrees awarded, rather than faculty or students currently enrolled.

## What fifty philosophy graduates means

Fifty or sixty bachelor’s degrees in a year is a substantial philosophy graduating class. A national departmental survey estimated a median of eight bachelor’s degrees awarded per department in 2022–2023, and a median of eight faculty members in fall 2023. Among departments awarding PhDs, the average new doctoral intake was 4.8 students in fall 2023. [American Academy departmental survey](https://www.amacad.org/humanities-indicators/philosophy-departments-profile)

Those figures describe different aspects of size. Princeton describes a department with around twenty faculty and forty-five graduate students, graduating twenty to thirty undergraduate majors each year. Its twenty bachelor’s awards in our 2024 table therefore represent a graduating class, rather than the entire department. [Princeton’s description](https://philosophy.princeton.edu/about/present-and-future)

Program definitions also matter. USC reported fifteen bachelor’s awards under the single general Philosophy code in 2024, plus sixty-one under Philosophy, Other. Its combined philosophy-family total is seventy-six. The School of Philosophy offers interdisciplinary majors alongside its general philosophy degree, including Philosophy, Politics, and Law and Philosophy, Politics, and Economics. Counting only the general code would omit much of the school’s reported degree activity. [USC programs](https://dornsife.usc.edu/phil/programs-of-study/)

## UC Irvine combines influence with rapidly growing research output

UC Irvine combines a high influence score with growth in both published research and doctoral degree production. Its indexed articles increased from sixty to 116 between the two five-year publication periods, a rise of 93.3%.

Its philosophy-family research doctorates averaged six per year in 2018–2020 and 11.3 in 2022–2024, an increase of 88.9%. Bachelor’s awards averaged 30.3 and 35.3 in the same periods. These three-year averages smooth the fluctuations of individual graduating classes. The doctoral counts include both general Philosophy and Philosophy, Other, covering the two reported classifications rather than counting just one.

The university has distinct research communities in Philosophy and in Logic and Philosophy of Science. James Owen Weatherall's work investigates the foundations of physics and scientific models. His paper on whether two formulations of Newtonian gravity are theoretically equivalent has 164 indexed citations in the snapshot; his work on the Hole Argument has 156.

Weatherall and Cailin O'Connor also investigate how false beliefs spread through communities. Their book, The Misinformation Age, connects philosophical and scientific questions about evidence with the information problems societies face. UCI describes their work on misinformation as including a National Science Foundation-funded study. [Weatherall's faculty profile](https://www.faculty.uci.edu/profile/?facultyId=5909), [UCI's misinformation research](https://www.socsci.uci.edu/newsevents/news/2021/2021-08-26-experts-on-misinformation.php)

This makes Irvine worth investigating for students interested in philosophy of science, scientific reasoning and the social organisation of knowledge.

## NYU shows the influence of research on knowledge and inquiry

NYU ranks fourth in this index and first in the Philosophical Gourmet Report's 2024 faculty-reputation ranking for the English-speaking world. The two measures supply different evidence: one follows citations to published scholarship, while the other asks philosophers to assess faculty quality. [Faculty-reputation results](https://philosophicalgourmet.com/overall-rankings-2024/)

Jane Friedman's work provides a concrete example of the scholarship behind NYU's citation result. In the collected snapshot, Why Suspend Judging? has 303 indexed citations, The Epistemic and the Zetetic has 278, and Inquiry and Belief has 267. These works address how belief, uncertainty and the activity of finding answers relate to one another. Friedman identifies epistemology and inquiry as central to her research. [Friedman's research](https://jfriedmanphilo.github.io/)

NYU's output in this journal collection declined from 104 articles to 86 between the two periods. An established research leader can remain influential without leading the growth table.

## Princeton has one of the strongest combinations of influence and publication growth

Princeton ranks eighth, with indexed article output increasing from 50 to 105, or 110.0%. That places it among the strongest growth cases near the top of the influence table.

The scholarship represented includes work on moral decision-making, language, belief and agency. Papers in the snapshot include Delia Graff Fara's Names Are Predicates, Victoria McGeer's Scaffolding Agency, and Mark Budolfson's examination of an objection to consequentialism. These records describe scholarship associated with Princeton during the publication period, including researchers whose appointments subsequently changed.

The longer degree series shows doctoral awards averaging 6.7 per year in 2018–2020 and 8.7 in 2022–2024. Bachelor’s awards averaged thirty-two and twenty respectively. Princeton’s publication growth therefore accompanied higher doctoral output and a smaller undergraduate graduating cohort. [IPEDS completions data](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/use-the-data)

## Oxford leads on the scale of influential scholarship

Oxford's influence score of 76.5 is more than twice the next university's score. Its indexed articles increased from 259 to 317 between the two periods.

Digital ethics contributes to that result. The collected records include research on algorithmic accountability, digital sovereignty and the relationship between AI systems and ethical principles. Oxford's broader philosophy community also includes its Institute for Ethics in AI, which connects philosophers and other humanities researchers with developers and users of AI. [Oxford's AI ethics research](https://philosophy.site.ox.ac.uk/ethics-in-ai)

There is an identifiable investment behind that research community. Oxford announced an inaugural institute team of seven philosophers, with John Tasioulas appointed as its first director. That is a documented expansion of research capacity alongside the university's publication record. [Oxford's appointment announcement](https://www.philosophy.ox.ac.uk/article/inaugural-academic-team-appointed-oxford-universitys-institute-ethics-ai)

## Rutgers brings a strong faculty reputation to foundational philosophy

Rutgers ranks seventh in the research index and third in the Philosophical Gourmet Report's 2024 English-speaking-world faculty ranking.

Jonathan Schaffer's Grounding in the Image of Causation has 552 indexed citations in the snapshot. His research asks what is fundamental and how other aspects of reality depend on it. Rutgers lists metaphysics, epistemology and language among his specialties, alongside a faculty working across mind, physics, ethics and cognitive science. [Schaffer's faculty profile](https://philosophy.rutgers.edu/people/regular-faculty/regular-faculty-profile/182-regular-faculty-full-time/625-schaffer-jonathan)

Rutgers' indexed articles declined from 104 to 82 between the two periods. Its place in the influence table rests on the scholarship's citation performance rather than a claim of recent expansion.

## Data and method

The journal snapshot was collected on 23 October 2026 through OpenAlex. Journals were fixed by ISSN before ranking. Articles span 2015-2024; citations are those indexed at collection. The most-cited tenth is calculated separately for each journal and publication year, with shared credit at citation ties and equal institutional credit across a paper's education-type affiliations. Indexed citation counts include self-citations. Institutions need at least 50 indexed articles. Fractional citation totals break equal influence scores.

The original source did not assign an education-type affiliation to 5,744 of the 23,457 articles. We also reviewed 25 works with acknowledgment-like affiliation strings, withheld unsupported institution credits and recorded each correction in the public affiliation audit. Those articles remain in the citation reference groups but cannot contribute university credit. This is a ranking of indexed institutional contributions, rather than a census of current philosophy faculty.

Sensitivity checks remove the two technology journals and, separately, Synthese. Oxford remains first in both checks. UC Irvine stays second without the technology journals and fourth without Synthese; NYU moves to third and second respectively. Some lower positions depend more strongly on journal selection.

U.S. degree figures use the source’s complete revised release when supplied, for award years 2018–2024. We sum detailed codes within CIP family 38.01, covering philosophy, logic, ethics and related philosophy programs, while excluding religious studies, clinical bioethics and joint degrees classified outside that family. First majors are counted; second majors and minors are outside these figures. Three-year averages require all three reported years. These are award counts, not enrollment or current faculty counts.

Faculty reputation is a separately attributed benchmark and does not enter the citation score. Publication growth does not establish that research influence caused an institution to expand. The accompanying data identify the institutional matches and each underlying subject code.
