# Test-Optional Scholarships: When ACT Scores Still Matter

A college can offer scholarships without test scores and still reward a particular ACT result. For a student deciding whether to retake the exam, the useful question is whether the next score threshold changes the funding available under their GPA and residency conditions.

Two Kentucky public universities show why the answer depends on the application. Their fall-2027 scholarship grids include test-optional awards alongside score-based routes. At particular thresholds, one additional ACT point changes the published annual tier by **$3,000 at the University of Kentucky** and **$2,000 at Eastern Kentucky**.[¹](#source-1)[²](#source-2)

## Two thresholds, with different eligibility

| University and award | Residency and unweighted GPA | ACT change | Published annual tier | Annual difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kentucky, Bluegrass | Nonresident; 3.50 | 29 to 30 | $13,000 to $16,000 | **$3,000** |
| Eastern Kentucky, President's to Regent's | On-campus Kentucky resident; 3.80 | 27 to 28 | $7,000 to $9,000 | **$2,000** |

At UKY, GPA 3.50 and ACT 25 already meet the $13,000 Bluegrass criteria. Reaching ACT 30 meets the $16,000 criteria. The university automatically considers eligible admitted students, but awards depend on early application and available funds. Meeting a published threshold does not guarantee the scholarship.[¹](#source-1)

At EKU, the $7,000 President's award requires no test. A student with the same 3.80 GPA and ACT 28 meets the $9,000 Regent's criteria; SAT 1310 is an alternative. The awards replace each other. They do not stack, and the lower amount in our comparison comes from the no-test route.[²](#source-2)[³](#source-3)

## The same score improvement can add nothing to the tier

Change the UKY applicant's GPA to **3.80**, and the calculation changes. The published test-optional route already reaches **$16,000 annually**. ACT 30 reaches that same Bluegrass tier. A student pursuing this award should check the GPA route before treating another ACT point as a funding opportunity.[¹](#source-1)

UKY's no-test Bluegrass criteria step from $6,500 at GPA 3.00 to $10,000 at 3.50, $13,000 at 3.60 and $16,000 at 3.80. That makes the transcript central to the decision, even when a score-based route is available.

## Check the conditions before booking another test

Both universities exclude ACT superscores. EKU also requires FAFSA and on-campus eligibility. Its published renewal GPA is 3.2 for President's and Regent's, with full-time enrollment and 24 earned EKU hours. The linked terms allow eight continuous semesters. UKY describes Bluegrass as four-year renewable, requiring at least 12 undergraduate hours each semester and a 3.0 cumulative GPA.[¹](#source-1)[²](#source-2)[³](#source-3)

UKY requires the completed admission application and supporting documents by **December 1, 2026**, and warns that funds can run out earlier. EKU lists March 1 for admission consideration, June 1 for acceptance and June 30 for updated transcripts or scores.[¹](#source-1)[²](#source-2)[³](#source-3)

Start with your residency, unweighted GPA and the university's accepted score rules. Identify a threshold that changes a relevant award, then use an [ACT diagnostic and preparation plan](/exams/act) to assess the work needed. If your existing GPA already reaches the same tier without a score, account for that before investing in another sitting.
